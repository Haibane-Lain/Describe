# OCR measurement: the reading bucket, measured

Generated by `train.ocr.measure` on the VizWiz-VQA **validation** split, with
`runs/ocr_easyocr/measure_val_reading.json` as the machine-readable record. This
document is the answer to `ocr-plan.md` §3 ("produce a number before any accuracy
target") and §6 Phase 3 (the combined caption-or-OCR figure).

**The headline: OCR alone reaches 11.4% on the reading bucket. The captioner
reaches 11.7%. Together they reach 20.3%.** OCR does not rescue the bucket on its
own — but it is not redundant either, because the two components fail on
*different* images.

---

## 1. Headline result

| Population | n | Caption | OCR | Either | Both |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — every answerable reading question | **577** | — | **11.4%** (66) | — | — |
| **B** — A, restricted to rows with a caption | **575** | **11.7%** (67) | **11.5%** (66) | **20.3%** (117) | **2.8%** (16) |

Two populations, because the captioner's prediction file omits two reading
images. Both are labelled wherever they appear; §2 explains why.

The difference between the captioner's 11.7% and OCR's 11.4% is **one question
out of 575** and is noise. The difference between either of them and the combined
20.3% is 50 questions, and is not.

---

## 2. Method

### The test set

577 answerable reading questions over 577 images, all present on disk, one
question per image (`train/ocr/reading_set.py`, which is the single definition of
the set). The classifier and the answer rule are **imported, not reimplemented**:
`is_reading_question` and `classify_kind` from `train/finetune/reading_gap.py`,
`majority_answer` and `answer_covered` from `train/finetune/eval_vqa.py` — the
same functions behind the published 11.7% and the consistent intent table.

**Validation that the two components are on the same basis:** recomputing the
captioner's per-subtype rates through this pipeline reproduces `ocr-plan.md` §4
exactly, subtype for subtype (9.6%, 8.8%, 13.8%, 13.5%, 17.9%, 25.0%, 0.0%,
40.0%, 25.0%, 0.0%, 0.0%). The comparison below is therefore like-for-like.

### The two populations, and the 577-versus-575 discrepancy

`ocr-plan.md` reports 577 images in §3 and a 575-question subtype table in §4,
without saying that these are different populations. The cause is measurable:
the captioner's `runs/blip_large_head/predictions_full_beam5.jsonl` covers 7,542
images and omits 75 validation question-images, **two of which are reading
questions**:

| Image | Question | Majority answer | Subtype |
|---|---|---|---|
| `VizWiz_val_00001985.jpg` | "What's on the screen?" | `black` | sign / screen text |
| `VizWiz_val_00002169.jpg` | "What is visible on this screen, please?" | `nothing` | sign / screen text |

That is exactly the 190-versus-188 difference in the sign/screen row. Both
answers are scene properties rather than printed text, so OCR cannot reach them
by construction; they belong in OCR's denominator (population A) but not in any
caption comparison (population B).

### Configuration, and how it was chosen

The frozen configuration is `train/configs/ocr_frozen.json`: EasyOCR 1.7.2 with
`canvas_size=1600`, `mag_ratio=1.0`, detector thresholds 0.6 / 0.35 / 0.35.

It was selected on the **train** split — 400 reading questions, never the 577 —
from five configurations:

| Screen config | Hits / 400 | Rate | No text | s/run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2560 / 1.5 / EasyOCR defaults, no preprocessing | 45 | 11.2% | 73 | 3.36 |
| 1600 / 1.5 / defaults, no preprocessing | 42 | 10.5% | 78 | 0.71 |
| 1600 / 1.0 / defaults + geometry | 44 | 11.0% | 76 | 0.69 |
| 1600 / 1.0 / lenient, no preprocessing | 46 | 11.5% | 74 | 0.61 |
| **1600 / 1.0 / lenient + geometry (frozen)** | **46** | **11.5%** | **71** | **0.70** |

**Only one finding here is statistically solid: canvas 2560 costs 4.8× the time
for no measurable accuracy gain.** The spread across all five is 42–46 hits
against a standard error of about 1.6 percentage points, so the configurations
are indistinguishable on accuracy. The frozen choice takes the joint-highest hit
count, the fewest images with no text at all, and keeps the geometry
preprocessing that `build-plan.md` §2.4 promises. The selection is recorded with
its reasoning inside `train/configs/ocr_frozen.json`.

`measure.py` refuses engine-parameter overrides on `--split val` unless a frozen
`--config-file` is supplied, so a configuration chosen by looking at the 577
cannot be passed off as a measurement.

### Protocol

* Input: 577 validation images, unmodified apart from conditional preprocessing.
* Text: all recognised regions concatenated in reading order.
* Scoring: `answer_covered(ocr_text, majority_answer)` — lowercase alphanumerics,
  stopwords dropped, remaining terms compared **in order** as a contiguous
  phrase, so `dog years` is not credited by a page containing "years" and "dog"
  separately.
* Only `answerable == 1` rows with a majority answer are scored.

## 3. Per subtype

Population A `n` for the OCR column; caption and combined are on population B.
Rates below n = 20 are printed by the tool with a "do not publish" marker and are
listed here only for completeness — `ocr-plan.md` §4 forbids building on them.

| Subtype | n | OCR | Caption | Either | Publish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sign / screen text | 190 | 11.1% | 9.6% | 17.6% | yes |
| label / packaging text | 136 | 8.8% | 8.8% | 16.9% | yes |
| other reading | 130 | 12.3% | 13.8% | 23.8% | yes |
| product / brand name | 52 | 15.4% | 13.5% | 25.0% | yes |
| book / media title | 28 | 10.7% | 17.9% | 25.0% | yes |
| phone / number | 16 | 31.2% | 25.0% | 37.5% | **no** |
| expiry / date | 14 | 7.1% | 0.0% | 7.1% | **no** |
| price / cost | 5 | 0.0% | 40.0% | 40.0% | **no** |
| instructions | 4 | 0.0% | 25.0% | 25.0% | **no** |
| address / location | 1 | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | **no** |
| medicine / dosage | 1 | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | **no** |

The two dominant subtypes — sign/screen and label/packaging, 326 of 577 (56%) —
are where OCR is weakest in absolute terms (11.1% and 8.8%) and where the
combined number gains least in relative terms. They are the honest targets.

Preprocessing made no difference to the total: the `enhance` pipeline also scored
66/577, moving one hit from "other reading" to "sign / screen text". Geometry
correction (upscaling 61 images under 1000 px, deskewing where the angle is
between 1.5° and 15°, perspective only for a ≥40%-frame quad) did not change the
answer, and cost nothing measurable in time (2.288 s/run against 2.259 s/run).

## 4. The combined number is the finding

| | Count | Share of 575 |
|---|---|---|
| OCR hit, caption missed | 50 | 8.7% |
| Caption hit, OCR missed | 51 | 8.9% |
| Both hit | 16 | 2.8% |
| **Either** | **117** | **20.3%** |
| Both missed | 458 | 79.7% |

The two components are almost perfectly **complementary in their failures**:
OCR answers 50 questions the captioner cannot, and the captioner answers 51 that
OCR cannot, while only 16 questions are answered by both. A union of two 11.5%
components yields 20.3%, not 11.5%.

This is the number the project should report for the reading bucket, and it
changes the ownership statement: **printed text is not "owned" in the sense of
being solved.** OCR is the right component to *hold* the responsibility and to be
improved, but at 11.4% it does not currently cover the bucket, and the captioner
must not be retired from it either.

---

## 5. Where the difficulty actually is

### Detection, not just precision

Of the 511 misses, the closest same-length window of recognised text is:

| Closeness to the answer | Misses | Share |
|---|---|---|
| No text recognised at all | 68 | 13.3% |
| Within 0.1 CER | 21 | 4.1% |
| Within 0.25 CER | 31 | 6.1% |
| Within 0.5 CER | 76 | 14.9% |
| Beyond 0.5 CER | 315 | 61.6% |

**A quarter of the misses (128 of 511) are within half a phrase of the answer** —
that is the headroom a better recogniser or decoder would recover, and it is the
"character-level accuracy on dense small text" that `ocr-plan.md` §1 predicted
would be the core difficulty. But the larger bucket is not near-miss: 62% of
misses are beyond 0.5 CER, and 68 images yield no text whatsoever. A single
dropped letter is enough to lose a question — `Mission TortiLA` against the
answer `mission tortilla strips` is a miss, correctly, under the same rule the
captioner is scored by.

### The quality gate is doing its job

| Split | n | OCR | Caption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate says recognizable | 568 | 11.6% | 11.8% |
| Gate says **unrecognizable** | 9 | **0.0%** | **0.0%** |
| Crowdworkers saw text (`text_detected`) | 549 | 12.0% | 11.1% |
| Crowdworkers saw no text | 28 | **0.0%** | 23.1% |

The 9 images the quality gate flags are read by neither component — evidence for
`ocr-plan.md` §8's request to "coordinate so OCR is not blamed for images the
gate should have caught". Conversely, the 28 images where crowdworkers reported
no text are unreachable for OCR by definition, and the captioner's 23.1% there
comes from answering about the scene, not the text.

The pattern `ocr-plan.md` §5 describes is exactly what the measurement shows: the
captioner identifies the object, OCR reads the words.

## 7. Latency

Measured on the demonstration hardware (RX 9070 XT, ROCm, MIOpen disabled):

| Workload | Mean | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Validation image (1.25 MP median) | **2.25 s** | per image, one pipeline |
| Train subset (0.31 MP typical) | 0.70 s | the screen's numbers |
| Preprocessing overhead | ~30 ms | geometry steps are not the cost |

The cost tracks image size and the number of regions found, not preprocessing. At
2.25 s per image, OCR is the slowest stage in the pipeline and the number the
proposal's per-stage latency budget has to accommodate.

## 8. What this changes

1. **`ocr-plan.md` §4 is a population-B table.** Its 188/575 is correct for the
   575 rows with captions; the full reading bucket is 190/577. Both are now
   labelled rather than mixed.
2. **The reading bucket is covered at 20.3%, not by either component alone.**
   §9 criterion 3 can be stated with numbers:

   | Intent | n | Owner, with basis |
   |---|---|---|
   | colour | 344 | captioner — 52.0% |
   | identify object | 804 | captioner — 44.0% |
   | other | 1,185 | captioner — 21.6% |
   | reading / detail | 577 | **neither alone** — caption 11.7%, OCR 11.4%, either 20.3% |
   | count | 15 | **nobody, and not publishable** (n < 20) |

3. **OCR is not a drop-in fix for the captioner's worst bucket.** It is a
   complementary reader: it adds 8.7 points of coverage the captioner lacks, and
   the captioner retains 8.9 points OCR lacks.
4. **The quality gate should run first.** The 9 gate-flagged images are 0% for
   both components; routing them to a retake is better than either reading.
5. **Next steps, in order of measured headroom:** the 68 images with no text at
   all (detection), then the 128 near-misses (recognition precision on dense
   small text), then the two dominant subtypes (sign/screen, label/packaging).
   Tuning belongs on the 1,980 train reading questions, never on the 577.

---

## 9. Limits

* **n = 577.** Overall differences below about 4 percentage points are inside the
  noise; single-digit differences are not findings. Six subtypes have n < 20 and
  no rate is published for them.
* **Ground truth is a crowdworker majority, not verified text.** Some "misses"
  are unreadable or mislabelled photographs; the strict phrase rule makes the
  metric unforgiving in both directions.
* **This measures one engine at one frozen configuration.** It is a measurement of
  the component as configured, not a ceiling for OCR on this data.
* **The collected label/document set for proposal §5C does not exist yet**, so
  word-level CER/WER on purpose-taken text photographs is still unmeasured. The
  harness for it is `train/ocr/word_metrics.py`.
* **Not for safety-critical decisions.** Medication names, dosages and expiry
  dates are in this bucket. The measurement above is a coverage rate, not a
  correctness guarantee, and the proposal's exclusion of safety-critical use
  stands.

## 10. Reproduce

```powershell
$env:TEMP = "$PWD\.tmp"; $env:TMP = "$PWD\.tmp"

py -3.12 -m train.ocr.smoke_test_ocr                      # 24/24 capability checks
py -3.12 -m train.ocr.measure --split val --pipelines none,enhance `
    --config-file train/configs/ocr_frozen.json `
    --combined runs/blip_large_head/predictions_full_beam5.jsonl `
    --out-dir runs/ocr_easyocr --resume

# the same numbers through the project's existing scorer, unchanged
py -3.12 -m train.finetune.intent_table `
    --predictions runs/ocr_easyocr/predictions_val_reading.jsonl `
    --out runs/ocr_easyocr/intent_table_ocr.json
```

Artifacts in `runs/ocr_easyocr/`:

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `measure_val_reading.json` | every rate with its population and n, per subtype, splits by gate verdict and `text_detected`, near-miss analysis, config provenance |
| `regions_val_reading.jsonl` | one row per (image, pipeline): text, regions with boxes and confidences, latency, preprocessing flags |
| `predictions_val_reading.jsonl` | `{"file_name", "prediction"}` — consumed by `intent_table.py` with no new code |
| `intent_table_ocr.json` | the independent cross-check: 577 scored, 66 hits, 11.4% |

The `--resume` flag makes the report regenerable from the saved rows without
re-running OCR, which is how this document's numbers can be re-derived in
seconds.
